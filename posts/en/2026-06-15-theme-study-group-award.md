## 01 | Why this deserved a standalone post

Yes, this experience could have been folded into my Yuan Ze exchange reflection and mentioned in only a sentence or two. I still wanted to record it separately, because it condensed a different kind of effort.

It was not simply “joining an activity.” It was a sustained process of reading, discussion, and viewpoint formation. Across six sessions, we kept returning to the same line of questions: how to think about money, risk, assets, ability, consumption, and future planning—and how to discuss them in relation to real life.

## 02 | What we discussed across the six sessions

The series did not revolve around copying notes from one book. Each session pushed the discussion forward.

| Session | Date | Main topics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2026-03-19 | Credit cards, whether self-occupied housing counts as an asset, and why many people struggle to accumulate wealth |
| 2 | 2026-03-26 | Currency appreciation, investment automation, and whether investment success depends more on knowledge or discipline |
| 3 | 2026-03-30 | Poverty versus bankruptcy, what we would do with money, and whether wealth should be built through investing or capability growth |
| 4 | 2026-04-09 | Continued discussion on wealth mentality and life planning |
| 5 | 2026-04-23 | Standards of wealth, whether extreme frugality is worthwhile, and whether mortgages mean “working for the bank” |
| 6 | 2026-05-07 | Entrepreneurship barriers, choosing between being a boss or an employee, and a full-series reflection |

Underneath these topics was a deeper training goal: learning how to think about real issues through return, cost, risk, time, and choice.

## 03 | How it connected back to Engineering Economics

What mattered most to me was that the study group was not isolated. It formed a continuous chain with my Engineering Economics coursework:

- the course offered the analytical frame,
- the books offered life-based cases,
- and the study-group discussions pushed ideas from “I know this” to “I can explain, compare, and express this.”

That is why the activity did not stay at the level of “completing a task.” It helped me carry concepts such as opportunity cost, cash flow, risk tolerance, and decision logic into actual discussion and expression.

## 04 | A visible result: second prize and NT$ 8,000 before tax

At the end of the program, our team won second prize in the 1142 Theme-based Study Group. That is a clear, direct result that does not need embellishment. It shows that sustained reading and discussion can eventually turn into a public, documentable outcome.
