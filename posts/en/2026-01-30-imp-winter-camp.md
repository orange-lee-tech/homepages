Thirteen talks, conversations with researchers, and on-site visits helped me connect nuclear physics, accelerator engineering, and sustainable energy within one engineering picture.</p>

## From nuclear physics to major scientific infrastructure

The talks moved from foundational nuclear-physics questions to heavy-ion therapy, material irradiation, nuclear-pore membranes, advanced-device reliability, and applications of accelerator technology. The most valuable shift for me was seeing how basic research, engineering systems, and social applications meet inside major scientific facilities.

## Linear accelerators and thermal engineering

A linear-accelerator lecture introduced high-power beams, advanced nuclear-energy operation, superconducting RF, controls, room-temperature acceleration, and cryogenic engineering. Follow-up conversations helped me understand that energy and power engineering can connect with thermal management, low-temperature systems, material reliability, and stable operation of large facilities.

## Entering the facility

The visit turned diagrams into physical systems: tunnels, beam paths, interfaces, safety interlocks, and test halls. I became more interested in how multiple engineering disciplines are coordinated to keep a complex facility reliable.

## Why this belongs in my undergraduate archive

The camp did not turn me into a nuclear physicist overnight. It changed the scale at which I view energy problems. Nuclear-waste treatment, irradiation testing of energy materials, accelerator thermal engineering, and reliable operation of major facilities are all part of energy security and engineering autonomy. I therefore record the camp as a direction-forming experience rather than merely an event attendance certificate.
